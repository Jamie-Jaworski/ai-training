
I found it. The slide you’re thinking of appears to be in the June AI Committee deck: AI Committee Meeting - June 2026 Final w-supplements.pdf.

Best match: Slide 14, “GOVERNANCE: Proposed Agentic AI Governance Tiers.” It presents a five-tier framework for matching governance requirements to AI application risk, with oversight increasing based on sensitive data access, broader sharing, recurring process support, regulated/customer-impacting outcomes, and action-taking capability.

The five tiers listed on that slide are:

Tier 1: Everyday AI Use / Personal Agents
Tier 2: Low-Risk Knowledge Agents
Tier 3: Human-Reviewed Process Support
Tier 4: High-Impact / Regulated Decision Support
Tier 5: Autonomous / Action-Taking Agents

The same deck also has the detailed follow-on slides that describe the characteristics of those tiers: Slide 25, “GOVERNANCE: Characteristics of Tiers 1-3 of AI Agents,” and Slide 26, “GOVERNANCE: Characteristics of Tiers 4-5 of AI Agents.” Slide 25 describes Tiers 1-3, including everyday productivity use, low-risk knowledge agents, and material process support / human-reviewed workflow assistance. Slide 26 describes Tier 4 high-impact / regulated decision support and Tier 5 autonomous / action-taking agents, including examples such as SAR drafting, sanctions monitoring, customer relationship recommendations, autonomous loan approval, automated SAR/CTR filing, customer account changes, transaction execution, and automated user access changes.

A useful shortcut: the executive summary confirms the deck was asking the Committee to approve this five-tier framework as FHB’s initial governance model for agentic AI, and to limit the initial Copilot full-license phase to Tier 1-3 unless Tier 4-5 cases were separately approved.
